DialUP finds UPnP/DLNA renderers on your Wi-Fi network and emulates each one as a cast target in the YouTube and YouTube Music apps. Pick a renderer in the cast menu, and the phone resolves the audio with yt-dlp, remuxes or transcodes it with ffmpeg and serves it to the renderer. No server is involved.

This app currently casts audio only.

Features:
• Renderers show up in the cast menu of YouTube and YouTube Music, with the controls you expect: seeking, volume, next and previous, autoplay, shuffle and repeat
• Add a renderer by its IP address or pair it with a code when your network blocks discovery
• Keeps playing when the YouTube app is killed or updated, and recovers from short Wi-Fi drops
• Gapless track changes and a prepared next track on renderers that support them
• Streaming, Direct or Cached playback, with a cache size you choose
• Hide or rename renderers
• No account, no analytics and no server of its own

Requirements: Android 8.0 or newer, a UPnP/DLNA renderer on the same network, and the YouTube or YouTube Music app.

DialUP downloads media through yt-dlp. When you turn on the automatic yt-dlp update, or manually update in Settings, it downloads a newer yt-dlp from GitHub. The automatic update is off by default. The yt-dlp that ships inside the app gets old, and YouTube can refuse its streams until it is updated.
